Before you jump

How it works

  1. Hold your phone flat against your chest with both hands, elbows tucked in. Keep it pressed there the whole time. Don't swing your arms.
  2. Stand still when you're told to. The phone uses this moment to weigh you.
  3. When you hear "Jump!", dip down and jump straight up as high as you can.
  4. Land on both feet, then stand still again until it says "Done". Then look at your results.
Safety: find a clear space on a flat, non-slip floor, with nothing above your head. Turn your volume up so you can hear the countdown.

What am I looking at?

The line shows how hard the ground pushed on you, in body weights. Standing still is 1. As you dip, you briefly "unweight" and it drops below 1. Then you push hard into the floor, often to 2–3 times your body weight. In the air it falls to zero, and landing gives a big spike.

Jump height comes from how long you were in the air. The longer you're up, the higher you went: height = g × time² ÷ 8.

Your phone gives an estimate. In our lab we measure this properly with force plates and motion capture, and that's what our students learn to do.
